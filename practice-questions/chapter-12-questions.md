**Q1.** A candidate is 20 minutes into the 220-1202 exam and encounters a complex drag-and-drop PBQ that is taking much longer than expected. Roughly 15 questions remain unanswered with 65 minutes left. What is the BEST action?

A) Keep working the PBQ until it is fully solved before moving on, since PBQs are worth more points
B) End the exam early and reschedule for a day with more preparation
C) Abandon the PBQ, submit a random answer, flag it, and continue to the remaining questions
D) Skip all remaining PBQs entirely and only answer multiple-choice questions

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: C**

PBQs do not carry extra weight over multiple-choice questions in the scoring model, so time spent on one question at the expense of many others is a poor trade-off. Flagging and submitting a best-guess answer preserves time for the rest of the exam.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- PBQs are not weighted higher than other questions, so sinking unlimited time into one is a losing strategy
- Ending the exam early forfeits the attempt and any correctly answered questions already completed
- Skipping PBQs without answering still leaves them blank; flagging with a guess is better since blank answers are always scored wrong

> ⚠️ Frequently missed on the exam — read the options carefully.

</details>

**Q2.** A candidate is scheduling their 220-1202 exam and wants to avoid traveling to a test center but is concerned about environment requirements. Which statement about online-proctored exams is accurate?

A) Online-proctored exams allow open notes as long as they are not read aloud during the exam
B) Online-proctored exams do not require ID verification since the proctor already has the candidate's registration data
C) Online-proctored exams allow a second monitor to be used for reference materials
D) Online-proctored exams require a room scan via webcam and a completely clear desk/workspace before the exam starts

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: D**

Online-proctored delivery requires the candidate to perform a 360-degree room scan and clear the desk/workspace of all materials, similar in strictness to a physical test center.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- No notes, papers, or reference materials are permitted in either format
- ID verification is still required and is typically done via webcam at the start of the session
- A second monitor is prohibited; only the single testing device/display may be used

> ⚠️ Frequently missed on the exam — read the options carefully.

</details>

**Q3.** A scenario-based question describes a customer's slow computer with multiple symptoms and offers four remediation steps as answers. The candidate is unsure of the exact best answer but can eliminate two options as clearly unrelated to the symptoms described. What should the candidate do next?

A) Compare the two remaining plausible options against the specific symptoms in the scenario and choose the one that addresses the most details, then flag it if still unsure
B) Re-read every answer option from scratch without using the eliminated options as a starting point
C) Guess randomly between all four original options since elimination doesn't improve odds enough to matter
D) Skip the question entirely and leave it blank, planning to return only if time allows

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: A**

Process of elimination narrows the field, then re-matching the remaining candidates against specific scenario details increases the odds of choosing correctly; flagging preserves the ability to revisit later without leaving it unanswered.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Ignoring the elimination work wastes the time already invested and doesn't leverage the narrowed field
- Random guessing among all four ignores useful information already gathered through elimination
- Leaving it blank guarantees it is scored wrong, whereas a flagged best-guess answer at least has a chance

</details>

**Q4.** The 220-1202 exam allows up to 90 questions in 90 minutes. On average, roughly how much time should a candidate budget per multiple-choice question to leave a reserve for PBQs and review?

A) About 30 seconds per question
B) About 60 seconds per question
C) About 3 minutes per question
D) About 5 minutes per question

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: B**

With 90 minutes for up to 90 questions, roughly 60 seconds per standard multiple-choice question is the working average, leaving extra minutes banked for PBQs which take longer.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- 30 seconds per question is too aggressive and leaves almost no time to read scenario-based questions carefully
- 3 minutes per question would only allow for about 30 questions total, far short of the full exam
- 5 minutes per question would allow only 18 questions in the full time, making it impossible to finish

</details>

**Q5.** PBQs on the 220-1202 exam typically appear near the beginning of the test. Which time-budgeting approach is recommended for handling them?

A) Skip all PBQs on first pass and return to them only if time remains at the very end
B) Allocate a larger block of time upfront (e.g., the first 10-15 minutes) specifically for PBQs since they take longer than multiple-choice questions
C) Spend no more than 10 seconds on each PBQ regardless of complexity
D) Answer PBQs last since they are worth fewer points than multiple-choice questions

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: B**

Because PBQs appear early and require more time to complete (simulations, drag-and-drop configuration steps), budgeting extra time upfront prevents the reserve time from being eaten into later in the exam.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Skipping PBQs to the end risks running out of time since the reserve was never planned for them
- 10 seconds is unrealistic for any PBQ that involves multiple configuration or ordering steps
- PBQs are not scored lower than multiple-choice questions, so deprioritizing them on that basis is a false assumption

> ⚠️ Frequently missed on the exam — read the options carefully.

</details>

**Q6.** A question on the exam includes the instruction phrase indicating more than one option must be chosen to be marked correct. Which action correctly identifies this question type?

A) Assume it is a standard single-answer question because the format looks the same as other questions
B) Select only the first option that seems correct and move on, since partial credit is given
C) Look for explicit instruction text such as 'select two' or 'choose all that apply' and select exactly that many options before submitting
D) Leave extra checkboxes unselected to avoid being penalized for wrong guesses

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: C**

Multiple-response questions explicitly state how many selections are required. Selecting the wrong number of options (too few or too many) typically results in the entire question being marked incorrect.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Assuming single-answer format when multiple selections are required leads to an automatically incorrect response
- No partial credit is given on CompTIA multiple-response questions; all required selections must be correct
- Leaving out required additional selections when the question calls for multiple answers results in the question being scored wrong

> ⚠️ Frequently missed on the exam — read the options carefully.

</details>

**Q7.** After completing the 220-1202 exam, a candidate receives a score report showing an overall scaled score along with domain-level breakdowns. What is the primary purpose of the domain-level breakdown on a failing score report?

A) It shows the exact questions missed so the candidate can memorize the correct answers before retaking
B) It identifies relative strength and weakness by domain so the candidate can focus retake preparation on the lowest-performing areas
C) It determines whether the candidate must wait a mandatory retake period, which varies by domain score
D) It replaces the overall pass/fail scaled score as the primary indicator of certification eligibility

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: B**

The domain-level breakdown gives relative performance across exam objectives, helping a candidate target study efforts on weaker domains without revealing specific missed questions.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Actual exam questions and answers are never disclosed on a score report due to exam security policies
- Retake waiting periods are governed by CompTIA's voucher/retake policy, not by domain score performance
- The overall scaled score (out of 900) is what determines pass/fail against the passing score threshold, not the domain breakdown

</details>

**Q8.** The 220-1202 exam uses a scaled scoring system ranging from 100 to 900. What is the minimum passing score a candidate must achieve on this exam?

A) 650
B) 700
C) 750
D) 800

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

The 220-1202 exam requires a scaled score of 700 out of a possible 900 to pass.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- 650 is below the actual published passing threshold for this exam
- 750 is higher than the actual required passing score
- 800 is higher than the actual required passing score and is often confused with other certification exams' thresholds
